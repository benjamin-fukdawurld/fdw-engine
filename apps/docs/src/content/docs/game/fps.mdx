A second game, in first person this time. The player stands in a walled
arena, at the end of the day; drones come in through the four gates, in
waves, and fly at the player to strike it. The weapon in the hand shoots
them down, three hits each. Each wave is bigger than the last; the game
ends when the health of the player falls to zero.

The game reuses what the maze built — static nodes, a tag `Solid`, the
collisions of circles, unlit materials, components with columns, a
scheduler — and adds what a first person game needs: a camera at the
height of the eyes, the pointer locked, a weapon that follows the view,
shots traced with rays, enemies drawn between two steps.

<Example name="fps" code={false} />

Click the arena, then **Play**: the **mouse** looks around, **W A S D**
walks, **Shift** runs, **Space** jumps, a **click** (or **F**, held for
automatic fire) shoots. **Escape** or **P** opens the menu: the arrows
choose, Enter or a click confirms. With a gamepad: the left stick to walk,
the right stick to look, the south button to jump, the right trigger to
shoot, Start for the menu.

## Where the code is

The code of the game is in `packages/demo-scenes/src/fps/`, shared by:

- the browser demo: `npx nx serve demo`, then the **FPS** route;
- the native demo: `npx nx run demo-native:start -- fps` (Escape quits the
  native demo: **P** pauses there);

## 1. The arena: a static level

The arena is 40 m across, centered on the origin. Everything in it is built
once, and never moves: every node is **static**, indexed once in the octree
of the scene.

```ts title="fps/arena.ts"
const Solid = scene.world.defineComponent('solid', {});
const level = scene.root.add({ name: 'arena', static: true });

const solid = (material, position, scale, name) =>
  level
    .add({ name, position, scale, static: true, occluder: true })
    .setMesh(cube, material)
    .attach(Solid);
```

- The floor is made of tiles of 2 m, in two shades: a scale for the eye, to
  feel the speed of walking. The tiles cast no shadow, and are not solid.
- The walls are 4 m high, with a gate of 3 m in the middle of each, and a
  dark recess behind it: the drones come out of the dark.
- Eight pillars in a ring and a score of crates give cover. The crates are
  placed by a seeded random generator, away from the four lanes from the
  gates: the drones always find their way in.
- What blocks is `Solid`, as in the maze: the player and the drones collide
  with the same entities. Marked `occluder` too, the walls, the pillars
  and the crates are ready for the
  [occlusion culling](/fdw-engine/tutorials/occlusion-culling/): they hide
  what is behind them.

The light is a **low sun**, casting long shadows across the floor, and four
lamps on posts at the corners — point lights, with a glowing bulb each. The
bulbs use the unlit `glowMaterial` of the maze (step 4): their color does
not depend on the lights. The game uses it for everything that glows — the
bulbs, the eyes of the drones, the muzzle flash, the sparks, the crosshair.

## 2. Two contexts of actions

The game reads two `ActionMap`s, as in
[First person controls](/fdw-engine/tutorials/first-person/): one for the
game, one for the menu. The game starts in the menu, so its map starts
disabled.

```ts title="fps/controls.ts"
const game = new ActionMap(
  input,
  {
    ...FlyActions,
    lift: { type: 'axis', bindings: [] },
    jump: { type: 'button', bindings: ['key:Space', 'gamepad:south'] },
    fire: {
      type: 'button',
      bindings: ['pointer:0', 'key:KeyF', 'gamepad:rightTrigger'],
    },
    // Escape quits the native demo: P pauses everywhere.
    pause: {
      type: 'button',
      bindings: ['key:Escape', 'key:KeyP', 'gamepad:start'],
    },
    locked: { type: 'button', bindings: ['pointer:locked'] },
  },
  { enabled: false },
);
```

- `FlyActions` are the actions the `FlyOperator` reads: `move`, `look`,
  `run`, `lift`. The game takes them all, and empties `lift`: a walker does
  not fly up and down. Bound to nothing, the axis stays at 0.
- `locked` is a button **down while the pointer is locked**. In a browser,
  Escape is the key that releases the lock: the game watches `locked`
  being released, and opens the menu then.

## 3. The player: a camera at the height of the eyes

The player is no body seen from afar: it is the **node of the camera**. A
`FlyOperator` in `walk` mode turns it with the mouse or the right stick,
and walks it horizontally, wherever it looks:

```ts title="fps/player.ts"
this.eye = scene.root.add({ name: 'eye' }).setCamera(camera);
this.fly = new FlyOperator(target, actions, {
  mode: 'walk',
  speed: 5,
  runFactor: 1.6,
  pointerSpeed: 2,
});
```

The operator moves a `CameraTarget`: an object with a `position`, a
`rotation` and a `projection`. Here, its position and rotation are those of
the transform of the node, and its projection that of the camera — the
operator reads the aspect, so that the pointer turns the view as much in
both directions.

```ts title="fps/player.ts"
const target: CameraTarget = {
  get position() {
    return eye.transform.position;
  },
  set position(position: Vec3Like) {
    eye.transform.position = position;
  },
  // … the same for rotation …
  get projection() {
    return camera.projection;
  },
};
```

The operator moves the eye; the player then corrects it. Each frame:

```ts title="fps/player.ts"
fly.update(dt); // looks, walks
if (this._actions.button('jump').pressed && this.grounded) {
  this._fall = jumpSpeed;
}
this._fall -= gravity * dt;
this._feet = Math.max(0, this._feet + this._fall * dt);

// A circle at the height of the knees: the crates stop it too.
probe.x = fly.position.x;
probe.y = this._feet + 0.5;
probe.z = fly.position.z;
resolveCircle(this._scene, this._solid, probe, this.radius);

fly.position.y = this._feet + eyeHeight;
fly.apply();
```

- **The jump** is a vertical speed, pulled down by gravity, until the feet
  are back at 0. `grounded` forbids a second jump in the air.
- **The collisions** are those of the maze (step 2): a circle of 40 cm,
  pushed out of the solid boxes near it, sliding along them. The circle is
  tested at the height of the knees, not of the eyes: a crate of 1 m
  stops the player, as a wall does. The circle rises with the feet: at the
  top of a jump, about 95 cm up, it passes over a small crate.
- The gates lead out, into the recesses: the position is also clamped to
  the inside of the arena.
- `fly.apply()` writes the position and the angles back into the node,
  after the corrections.

**The recoil.** At each shot, the view kicks up a little, then comes back
down, slower:

```ts title="fps/player.ts"
kick(amount: number): void {
  const pitch = Math.min(this.fly.pitch + amount, this.fly.maxPitch);
  this._recoil += pitch - this.fly.pitch;
  this.fly.pitch = pitch;
  this.fly.apply();
}
```

The recoil left is remembered apart: only what the shots added is taken
back, while the player keeps aiming with the mouse in between.

## 4. The weapon in the hand

The weapon is a few cubes — a body, a barrel, a grip, a glowing sight —
**children of the node of the camera**. The hierarchy does the rest: the
weapon follows every movement of the view, without a line of code.

```ts title="fps/weapon.ts"
export const ViewLayer = 1 << 1;

this.node = eye.add({ name: 'weapon', layers: ViewLayer });
this.node
  .add({ position, scale, layers: ViewLayer })
  .setMesh(cube, metal, { castShadow: false });
```

- About 25 cm from the eye, the weapon needs a camera that sees close: its
  `near` is 5 cm. The muzzle is 46 cm ahead, the player keeps 40 cm from
  the walls: against a wall, the barrel sinks into it.
- The weapon casts no shadow: it is part of the view, not of the arena.
- Each frame, it is placed in the lower right of the view, **swaying with
  the steps** — the distance walked, not the time, so it is still when the
  player stops — and kicked back after a shot.

**The flash.** At the muzzle, a glowing ball and a point light, hidden. A
shot shows them for 50 ms: the light lights the weapon, and the walls
close by.

```ts title="fps/weapon.ts"
trigger(): boolean {
  if (this._cooldown > 0) {
    return false;
  }
  this._cooldown = period; // 0.12 s: about 8 shots per second
  this._kick = 1;
  this._flash = flashTime;
  return true;
}
```

**The crosshair** is a small cube, also a child of the eye, half a meter
ahead, in the center of the view. A drone hit turns it red and bigger for a
moment: its node gets another material.

### What the player carries is on its own layer

The weapon, the crosshair and the sparks are on a **layer** of their own,
`ViewLayer`. The renderer draws every layer; the shots search all the
others:

```ts title="fps/game.ts"
this._scene.raycastEntities(ray, onHit, { layers: ~ViewLayer }, range);
```

Without it, the first thing every shot would hit is the weapon, or the
crosshair right in front of the eye, or the spark of the previous shot.

## 5. The drones: a component, and its systems

Each drone is a node — a body, a red eye, two wings, children of its node —
and an **entity** with a `drone` component:

```ts title="fps/drones.ts"
const droneSchema = {
  x: 'f32',
  y: 'f32',
  z: 'f32',
  // Where it was at the step before.
  px: 'f32',
  py: 'f32',
  pz: 'f32',
  health: 'f32',
  cooldown: 'f32', // before it can strike again
  flash: 'f32', // the white flash of a hit
  down: 'f32', // shot down: the time since; 0 while it flies
  phase: 'f32', // so that the drones do not bob together
  body: 'ref',
} as const;
```

The logic of the drones runs at the **fixed step**, in three systems that
go through the columns:

- `steer` flies each drone towards the player, at 3.2 m/s, pushed away
  from the drones closer than 1.6 m, out of the walls with the same
  `resolveCircle` as the player, bobbing up and down. Close enough, it
  stops and hovers.
- `strike`: a drone within 1.5 m of the player strikes it, for 12 points,
  then waits 1.2 s.
- `fade`: the white of a hit fades; a drone shot down falls, then its node
  is removed.

Removing a node removes its entity from the store. A loop that removes
entities goes over a copy:

```ts title="fps/drones.ts"
for (const e of [...this.Drone.entities]) {
  // …
  if (down[e] >= fallTime) {
    this._scene.node(e).remove();
  }
}
```

### Drawn between two steps

The logic moves the drones 60 times per second; the screen may draw 144
frames. Moved only at the steps, they would stutter. `steer` keeps the
position of the previous step (`px`, `py`, `pz`) before moving, and the
frame draws each drone **between the two**:

```ts title="fps/drones.ts"
draw(alpha: number, player: { x: number; z: number }): void {
  const { x, y, z, px, py, pz, down } = this.Drone;
  for (const e of this.Drone.entities) {
    const node = this._scene.node(e);
    const cx = px[e] + (x[e] - px[e]) * alpha;
    const cz = pz[e] + (z[e] - pz[e]) * alpha;
    node.transform.position = { x: cx, y: py[e] + (y[e] - py[e]) * alpha, z: cz };
    // Facing the player, rolling on its side when falling.
    const yaw = Math.atan2(-(player.x - cx), -(player.z - cz));
    node.transform.rotation = multiplyQuat(
      rotationAround('y', yaw),
      rotationAround('z', down[e] * 12)
    );
    // …
  }
}
```

`alpha`, from the scheduler, tells where the frame is between the last two
steps, from 0 to 1. The columns are the truth of the logic; the nodes, what
is drawn. [Systems and the fixed step](/fdw-engine/tutorials/systems/)
details the technique.

## 6. Shooting: a ray from the eyes

The trigger held and the weapon ready, a shot is a **ray** from the eyes,
along the view, to the first thing it meets:

```ts title="fps/game.ts"
shoot(): void {
  if (!this._actions.button('fire').down || !this.weapon.trigger()) {
    return;
  }
  const { position, forward } = this.player;
  // … the ray from position, along forward …
  let hit: { entity: number; distance: number } | undefined;
  this._scene.raycastEntities(
    ray,
    (entity, distance) => {
      hit = { entity, distance };
      return false; // the nearest hit is enough
    },
    { layers: ~ViewLayer },
    range
  );
  // …
}
```

- `button('fire').down`, not `pressed`: the trigger held fires again as
  soon as the weapon is ready — automatic fire, at the rate of `period`.
- The hits come nearest first: the first one stops the walk. A crate
  between the player and a drone takes the shot.
- `forward` is the direction of the view, from the angles of the operator:
  the ray goes through the crosshair.

The ray hits a **part** of a drone — its body, its eye, a wing — not the
node of the drone, which has no mesh. The drone is the parent of the part:

```ts title="fps/drones.ts"
droneOf(entity: number): number | undefined {
  const parent = this._scene.node(entity).parent;
  return parent &&
    this.Drone.has(parent.entity) &&
    !this.Drone.down[parent.entity]
    ? parent.entity
    : undefined;
}
```

Then:

- **A drone hit** flashes white (its body gets the glowing material for
  80 ms), the crosshair turns red; at the third hit, it is shot down, and
  the score grows by 100 times the number of the wave.
- **The arena hit**: a spark flies 5 cm before the surface, not inside it.
  The sparks are twelve glowing cubes, reused: the oldest moves to the new
  hit, shrinks and spins, then hides.
- Hit or miss, the view kicks up, and the gamepad rumbles: a little for a
  miss, more for a hit, most for a drone shot down.

## 7. The frame, with a scheduler

`fps.ts` puts everything into the four phases of a `Scheduler`:

```ts title="fps/fps.ts"
// begin: the events since the previous frame; the menu, the pause.
scheduler.add('input', readInputAndMenu, { phase: 'begin' });

// fixed: the drones, their blows, the waves; paused under the menu.
scheduler.add('drones', (step) => game.drones.steer(step, player.position), {
  phase: 'fixed',
});
scheduler.add('strikes', strikes, { phase: 'fixed', after: ['drones'] });
scheduler.add('fade', (step) => game.drones.fade(step), {
  phase: 'fixed',
  after: ['strikes'],
});
scheduler.add('waves', (step) => game.waves(step), {
  phase: 'fixed',
  after: ['fade'],
});

// update: the player at the rate of the screen; the drones drawn between
// their last two steps, then the shots, which hit what is drawn.
scheduler.add('player', (dt) => {
  if (actions.enabled) {
    player.update(dt);
  }
});
scheduler.add('draw', () => game.drones.draw(scheduler.alpha, player.position));
scheduler.add('shoot', () => game.shoot(), { after: ['player', 'draw'] });
scheduler.add('effects', effects, { after: ['shoot'] });

// render: the view; the line of the state.
scheduler.add('render', render, { phase: 'render' });
scheduler.add('status', status, { phase: 'render', after: ['render'] });
```

Two choices differ from the maze:

- **The player is not at the fixed step.** The view must follow the mouse
  at every frame: turned at 60 steps per second on a 144 Hz screen, it
  would judder at each movement of the hand. Walking goes with it, and the
  collisions are tested each frame, with the time of the frame — at most
  0.1 s, as `frame` caps it.
- **The shots come after `draw`.** The ray tests the nodes where they are
  drawn, between two steps: the player hits what they see, not where the
  logic was a fraction of a step ago.

## 8. The menu and the pause

The menu is open at the start, at a pause, at the end of a game. Opening
it switches the contexts, pauses the scheduler and releases the pointer:

```ts title="fps/fps.ts"
const play = () => {
  if (game.state === 'over') {
    game.reset();
  }
  started = true;
  menu.enabled = false;
  actions.enabled = true;
  scheduler.paused = false;
  // After a click or a key: the browser accepts the lock.
  input.lockPointer();
};
const pause = () => {
  actions.enabled = false;
  menu.enabled = true;
  scheduler.paused = true;
  selected = 0;
  input.lockPointer(false);
};
```

- `scheduler.paused` stops the `fixed` phase only: under the menu, the
  drones freeze, and the frame goes on — the input, the menu, the
  rendering.
- `play` is called from a click or a key: a browser only locks the pointer
  in answer to a gesture of the user.
- The menu has four entries: Play (Resume, Play again), the rumble, the
  inverted vertical axis, the sensitivity of the pointer. These settings
  are properties of the game and of the operator: `game.rumble`,
  `fly.invertY`, `fly.pointerSpeed`.

When the health of the player falls to 0, the `strikes` system ends the
game and opens the menu, at once: the steps left in the frame are dropped.

**The view tells the state.** The exposure of the rendering dims the arena
under the menu, and darkens it for a moment at each blow:

```ts title="fps/fps.ts"
renderer.render({
  source: scene,
  camera,
  surface,
  exposure: menu.enabled ? 0.35 : 1 - game.hurt * 2,
  clearColor: { r: 0.55, g: 0.45, b: 0.42, a: 1 },
});
```

The line under the canvas gives the wave, the drones flying, the health,
the score and the hits; under the menu, its entries, the selected one in
brackets.

## 9. The waves

When no drone is left — the last one fallen and removed — the game waits
two seconds, then sends the next wave:

```ts title="fps/game.ts"
const count = Math.min(1 + 2 * this.wave, maxDrones); // 3, 5, 7… 12
for (let k = 0; k < count; k++) {
  const gate = gates[k % gates.length];
  const offset = [0, -1.1, 1.1][Math.floor(k / gates.length) % 3];
  // …
  this.drones.spawn(x, z, k * 1.7);
}
```

The drones come in through the gates in turn — north first, ahead of the
player at the start — three abreast at most in each. Their phases differ,
so they do not bob together.

## 10. The sounds

The game tells what happens — a shot, a hit, a drone shot down, a blow, a
wave — to an `FpsSoundEvents`, given when there is an audio engine
(`context.audio`: a `WebAudioEngine` in the page, a `NativeAudioEngine` in
the native demo). `FpsSounds` plays them on the
[audio](/fdw-engine/tutorials/audio/) of the engine:

```ts title="fps/sounds.ts"
this.audio = new SceneAudio(scene, engine);
this.audio.listener = eye; // the ears of the player

this._shot = new SoundEffect(engine, shot, {
  bus: sfx,
  pitchVariation: 0.06, // never twice the same
  maxVoices: 6,
});

spawned(drone: Node): void {
  // A hum that follows the drone, each its pitch.
  this.audio.play(drone, this._hum, {
    loop: true,
    pitch: 0.9 + 0.2 * Math.random(),
    spatial: { refDistance: 2 },
  });
}
```

- **The effects** (the shot, the tick of a hit, the blow) are
  `SoundEffect`s: a pitch drawn at each play, a number of voices bounded.
- **The drones hum where they fly**: a voice per drone, attached to its
  node, heard from the eye; the hum fades out when the drone falls, and
  an explosion rings where it is.
- **The music** is a `MusicPlayer`: a calm loop under the menu, crossfaded
  to a faster one in the game, on a `music` bus at half the volume.
- The menu has a **Sound** entry: the master bus muted.

Every sound is **made by code** (`demo-scenes/src/audio/synth.ts`: noise,
sines, envelopes, a small sequencer for the music): nothing to load, the
same in the browser and in the native demo. In the page, the sound starts
at the first click.

## Testing it

The game runs without a page and without a GPU: `FpsGame` takes a scene, an
arena, a camera and an `ActionMap`, and a `FakeInputSource` drives the
actions. `fps/fps.spec.ts` walks the player into a wall, makes it jump,
shoots a drone down in three hits, shoots at the floor without hitting
what the player carries, lets the drones strike until the game is over,
and checks that a wave down brings a bigger one. On a `FakeAudioEngine`,
it checks that each drone hums where it flies, that the shots and hits
play, that a drone shot down explodes in the world, and that the music
crossfades when the game starts.

## What we have

- A level of static nodes, solid and occluding, lit by a low sun and lamps.
- A player that is a camera: a `FlyOperator` in `walk` mode, corrected for
  the jump and the collisions.
- A weapon made of children of the camera, on a layer of its own, that the
  shots do not see.
- Drones as entities, moved by systems at the fixed step, drawn between two
  steps.
- Shots traced with rays of the scene, from the parts hit back to the
  drone.
- A pause menu: two contexts of actions, the pointer locked and released,
  the scheduler paused.
- Sounds made by code: effects varied at each play, drones humming in
  space, a music crossfaded between the menu and the game.

Some ideas left for later: drones that shoot back, with rays of their own;
a weapon drawn in a pass of its own, so that it never sinks into a wall; a
minimap, as in the maze.
