A third game, seen over the shoulder. A ranger stands in a clearing of a
fir forest, under a low sun; orcs come out of the trees, in waves, then a
demon leads the last one. The ranger strikes with an estoc — three blows
in a row, or a heavy one — parries, rolls out of the way, jumps. The game
is won when the demon falls, lost when the ranger does.

The game puts together most of the engine: the forest is instanced, with
its levels of detail (P1), under shadows in cascades (P2), on a terrain of
patches (P3b), its trunks hiding what is behind them (P3); the camera
follows the ranger over its shoulder, the ranger is a character controller
(P4, P7b); the models and their animations come from glTF files (P5, P6),
the sounds are made by code (P7). It adds what a fight needs: blows as
volumes tested while the animations strike, and enemies decided by a
behavior graph (P9).

Click the forest, then **Play**: the **mouse** turns the camera, **W A S D**
(Z Q S D on AZERTY) moves, **Shift** sprints, a **click** strikes (click
again for the next blow), the **right button** or **E** strikes hard,
**F** held parries, **C** rolls, **Space** jumps. **Escape** or **P** opens
the menu. With a gamepad: the sticks to move and turn, the west button to
strike, the north one for a heavy blow, the left shoulder to parry, the
east button to roll, the south one to jump, Start for the menu.

## Where the code is

The code of the game is in `packages/demo-scenes/src/tps/`, shared by:

- the browser demo: `npx nx serve demo`, then the **TPS** route;
- the native demo: `npx nx run demo-native:start -- tps` (**P** pauses
  there, Escape quits);
- this page.

| File          | Contents                                                              |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `assets.ts`   | Loading the models and the textures, with the progress                |
| `forest.ts`   | The terrain, the firs and the ground cover, their levels and bodies   |
| `level.ts`    | The forest as a level of the engine: saved once, loaded by the game   |
| `actor.ts`    | A character: a controller, a model turned where it faces, animated    |
| `hero.ts`     | The ranger: its actions, its animation graph, its blade               |
| `combat.ts`   | The blows: volumes struck once per blow                               |

| `enemies.ts`  | The orcs and the demon: their bodies, what the nodes of the graph do  |
| `controls.ts` | The actions of the game and of the menu                               |
| `sounds.ts`   | The sounds, made by code                                              |
| `tps.ts`      | The `SceneFactory`: the menu, the waves, the frame run by a scheduler |

## 1. The models

Every model is **CC0**: free to use, change and share, without credit.

| Model                                                   | Author                               | Used for                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| Fir Tree 01, Fern 02, Grass Medium 01, Forest Ground 04 | [Poly Haven](https://polyhaven.com)  | The forest, the ground       |
| Antique Estoc                                           | [Poly Haven](https://polyhaven.com)  | The sword                    |
| Modular Character Outfits – Fantasy (Male Ranger)       | [Quaternius](https://quaternius.com) | The ranger                   |
| Universal Animation Library 1 and 2                     | [Quaternius](https://quaternius.com) | The animations of the ranger |
| Ultimate Monsters (Orc, Demon)                          | [Quaternius](https://quaternius.com) | The enemies                  |

They are not used as downloaded: the fir has 4 million triangles, the
ranger textures of 4096 px, its animations are in other files. Two tools
prepare them into compact `.glb` files, in `apps/docs/public/models/forest/`
(26 MB in all):

- `tools/assets/make-forest.mjs`: the firs in three levels of detail
  (see [Levels of detail](/fdw-engine/tutorials/levels-of-detail/)), the
  ferns and the grass simplified, made opaque (their leaves are geometry,
  not textures with alpha);
- `tools/assets/make-characters.mjs`: the ranger with its textures
  reduced, the clips it plays copied from the animation libraries **by the
  names of the joints** (the same skeleton), and the estoc in its right
  hand. Where the estoc sits is computed from the pose of the hand in the
  guard of `Sword_Idle`: the grip through the middle phalanges, the blade
  out of the thumb side, the guard along the fingers.

## 2. The forest

`buildForest` makes the level, from a seed:

- **A terrain** of 512 m, drawn by patches as in
  [Tessellation](/fdw-engine/tutorials/tessellation/), with heights of its
  own: a flat clearing, gentle bumps (a fbm of `@fdw/engine-noise`), and
  around the play area a rim too steep for the character to climb
  (`maxSlope` of 42°). The ground is the forest floor of Poly Haven,
  repeated every 4 m, its normal map over the normal of the height map; its
  repetition broken by noises of the world position (patches greener or
  drier, the scan read at a second scale); on the slopes, a rock baked from
  noises while the models load, read in triplanar mapping (see
  [A procedural terrain](/fdw-engine/tutorials/procedural-terrain/)).
- **2 500 firs**, at least 6 m apart, of three kinds, turned and scaled at
  random. Each kind, each level, each primitive is a single
  `scene.createBatch` for all its firs; `SceneLods` chooses their levels
  for the camera (30 m, 80 m, 320 m). The trunks are occluders.
- **Grass and ferns**, about 5 000 clumps, drawn within 30 to 40 m of the
  camera (a single level each).
- **Their bodies**: the terrain as a heightfield, from the same heights;
  the trunks near the play area as capsules.

The forest is built once, by `tools/assets/make-tps-level.ts`, and saved
as a level of the engine beside the models: `forest/forest.scene.json`
(the nodes, the batches and their levels of detail, the bodies, the
terrain and its material) and its binary (the transforms, the heights).
The game loads it (`createTpsScene({ level: TpsLevelPath })`) instead of
building it: the same forest, the same game, played the same. The terrain
and what the game needs of the forest are two components of the demo,
saved by codecs of its own (see
[Saving and loading scenes](/fdw-engine/tutorials/saving-scenes/)); the
rock baked at the start is defined in the store under the URI the level
names (`defineTexture`). With `--gltf`, the tool writes the forest in glTF
too, for other tools.

The renderer draws the scene and the terrain together, in about 40 calls
for the view and 200 with the four cascades:

```ts title="tps/tps.ts"
forest.terrain.surface.update(camera, surface);
renderer.render({
  source: scene,
  items: [forest.terrain.surface],
  camera,
  surface,
  fog,
  occlusion,
  prepare: (graph) => {
    hero.actor.addPass(graph);
    enemies.addPasses(graph);
  },
});
```

The sun has four cascades up to 110 m
([Shadow cascades and fog](/fdw-engine/tutorials/shadow-cascades/)); a fog
falling with the height hides the end of the forest, and what the levels
of detail leave out.

## 3. A character: `Actor`

The ranger and the enemies are `Actor`s: a body moved by a character
controller, its node under the root (drawn between two steps by
`ScenePhysics`), the model under it, turned where the character faces,
skinned and animated.

```ts title="tps/actor.ts"
this.body = scene.root.add({ position: center });
this.visual = this.body.add({ position: { y: -center.y }, scale });
this.instance = instantiate(model, scene, { parent: this.visual });
this.skinning = new Skinning(backend, this.instance, model);
this.animator = new Animator(graph, { skeleton, clips });
this.character = physics.addCharacter(this.body, {
  shape: Shapes.capsule(halfHeight, radius),
});
```

`move(step, velocity, offset)` adds the gravity and moves the controller
at the fixed step, by an `offset` more (what the animation moved it);
`face(direction, step)` turns the model; `animate(dt)`, at each frame,
plays the animator and poses the skin, `applyPose(pose)` poses it as given
(a ragdoll). The characters are in their own collision group (4), the
level in group 1: the ragdolls (group 2) fall on the level, and the
characters and the camera go through them.

## 4. The ranger: one action at a time

The ranger does one thing at a time: `move`, `air`, `roll`, `attackA`,
`attackB`, `attackC`, a recovery after the first two blows, `heavy`,
`block`, `hit`, `dead`. The game code decides the action; the animation
graph follows it, by a parameter:

```ts title="tps/hero.ts"
const move = g.blend1d(
  [g.clip('SwordIdle'), g.clip('Walk'), g.clip('Jog'), g.clip('Sprint')],
  [0, 1.6, 4.5, 7],
  g.param('speed'),
);
g.stateMachine({
  states: { move, air, roll, attackA /* … */ },
  transitions: HeroActions.map((action, index) => ({
    from: '*',
    to: action,
    conditions: [{ parameter: 'action', value: index }],
    duration: fadeOf(action),
  })),
});
```

The rules stay in code, where they are easy to read and to test:

- **The chain of blows.** A click during a blow queues the next one, which
  starts once the blade has struck (60 % of the clip); without it, the
  blow ends in its recovery, which moving or striking cuts short.
- **Facing the enemy.** As a blow starts, the ranger turns towards the
  enemy nearest in front within 4.5 m, else where the camera looks.
- **The lunge.** A blow carries the ranger forward by **root motion**: the
  clips of the library strike in place, so `withLunges` writes a step
  forward in the track of their `root` joint (35 to 70 cm, eased), and the
  animator takes it out of the pose (`rootMotion: { joint: 'root' }`, see
  [Root motion](/fdw-engine/tutorials/root-motion/)). The movement of each
  frame, turned where the ranger faces, is added to the next move of the
  controller: the lunge follows the blend of the clips, their speed, their
  cross-fades.
- **The roll** carries the ranger 4 m; for most of it, nothing hurts it.
- **The parry** takes 85 % of a blow off, from the front only.

## 5. The blows

The clips tell when the blade strikes: events added to them, at the
moments the hand moves fastest (measured on the clips):

```ts title="tps/hero.ts"
AttackA: { windows: [[0.15, 0.32]], damage: 20 },
AttackC: { windows: [[0.12, 0.3], [0.55, 0.72]], damage: 30 },
```

Between `strike` and `strikeEnd`, at each fixed step, the blade is a
**capsule** from the guard to the tip, tested against the bodies of the
enemies with `world.overlap`; the sweep of the tip since the previous step
is tested too: a fast blade crosses a body between two steps.

```ts title="tps/combat.ts"
export class Blow {
  readonly hit = new Set<PhysicsCollider>();
  constructor(
    readonly damage: number,
    readonly against: (collider: PhysicsCollider) => boolean,
  ) {}
}
```

A `Blow` remembers what it struck: a target is hit **once per blow**,
however many steps the blade stays in it. The enemies strike the same way,
with a sphere in front of them.

## 6. The enemies: a behavior graph

Each enemy is an `Actor` and an entity; what it does, a **behavior graph**
decides (see [Behavior graphs](/fdw-engine/tutorials/behavior-graphs/)):
the same graph for the orcs and the demon, an instance per enemy, their
differences in the variables given when attached.

```ts title="tps/enemies.ts"
this.behavior.attach(entity, {
  health: kind.health,
  reach: kind.reach,
  poise: kind.poise,
  rest: kind.cooldown,
  strikeStart: kind.strike[0],
  strikeEnd: kind.strike[1],
  attackLength: actor.clips['Weapon'].duration,
});
```

At each fixed step, `event.update` advances its clocks, then a switch on
its `state` variable decides: `spawn` (a roar), `chase`, `attack`,
`recover`, `hurt`, `dead`. It goes to `attack` when the hero is within
reach, its cooldown over, and fewer than two strike already; the others
wait. Then it steers (`enemy.steer`), and strikes during the window of its
blow (`enemy.strike`). A blow of the ranger is an event, `hit`, with its
damage: the health drops, the enemy falls, or staggers if the blow beats
its `poise` — the orcs at every blow, the demon at the heavy ones.

```ts title="tps/brain.ts"
const decide = g.switch(steps.exec('then1'), state, EnemyStates);
enter(after(decide.exec('spawn'), 0.8), 'chase');
const engage = g.branch(decide.exec('chase'), ready);
const attack = enter(engage.exec('true'), 'attack');
g.node('enemy.startAttack', { from: attack.then });
```

The graph decides; the game does the work its nodes ask for (`brain.ts`
declares them, `Enemies` implements them):

- `enemy.steer`: towards the hero, or for those waiting, towards their own
  place on a ring around it, along a **path** on the navigation mesh of
  the forest (a `NavAgent` each, a new path when the goal moved by 75 cm,
  three times a second at most); the velocity it would like, given to the
  **crowd**. Those striking hold their ground (`avoids: false`): the others
  go around them.
- `enemy.die`: the body leaves the physics, and a **ragdoll** takes over
  from the animation, pushed away from the blow: 13 bodies on the rig of
  the monsters, the limbs held in cones, the feet (moved by inverse
  kinematics, under the root of the rig) hung on the lower legs.
- `enemy.strike`: the blow, a sphere in front of the enemy, against the
  hero.
- `enemy.heroDistance`, `enemy.canAttack`, `enemy.swing`, `enemy.sink`,
  `enemy.remove`: the distance, the place among those striking, the
  sounds, the fallen sinking into the ground, then gone.

After the graph, a system moves them all: `crowd.update(step)` gives each
a velocity that runs into no other, as close as can be to the one it
wanted (see [Crowds and obstacles](/fdw-engine/tutorials/crowds/)), and
the controllers move.

```ts title="tps/navigation.ts"
buildNavMesh({
  bounds, // the play area
  volumes: trunks, // a capsule per trunk
  ground: forest.heightAt,
  cellSize: 0.5,
  agentRadius: 0.55, // the demon
  heightTolerance: 0.3,
});
```

The navigation mesh is built once, when the forest is ready: the ground of
the terrain (its height function), the trunks of the play area as
capsules, the floors of a polygon within 30 cm of each other — some 16 000
polygons for the play area of 100 m, in about 0.4 s. A path across it
takes about a millisecond. The waves come from places on the mesh, not in
a trunk.

The graph is compiled to JavaScript; `createTpsScene({ behavior:
'interpret' })` has it interpreted, for its debugger. Both cost about
0.2 ms a frame for a wave, mostly in the moves of the character
controllers.

## 7. The camera

A `ThirdPersonOperator` (see
[The third-person camera](/fdw-engine/tutorials/third-person-camera/)),
4.2 m behind the ranger, over its shoulder. Its probe casts a sphere in
the physics: the trunks, the ground and the enemies stop it, never the
ranger itself.

## 8. The frame

```ts title="tps/tps.ts"
// fixed: the ranger and its blows, the enemies, the crowd, the physics,
// the waves.
scheduler.add('hero', heroStep, { phase: 'fixed' });
// The graph of the enemies is a system.
enemies.behavior.install(scheduler, { name: 'enemies', after: ['hero'] });
scheduler.add('crowd', (step) => enemies.move(step), {
  phase: 'fixed',
  after: ['enemies'],
});
physics.install(scheduler, { after: ['crowd'] });
scheduler.add('waves', waves, { phase: 'fixed', after: ['physics'] });

// update: the bodies drawn between two steps, animated; the camera.
scheduler.add('animate', animate, { after: ['physics:sync'] });
scheduler.add('camera', followHero, { after: ['animate'] });

// render: the levels of detail for the camera, the sounds, the view.
forest.lods.install(scheduler, camera, { before: ['render'] });
```

The buttons pressed are kept until the next fixed step: a frame may run
none, or several. The menu pauses the fixed phase, as in
[the FPS](/fdw-engine/game/fps/).

## 9. The sounds

Made by code, as those of the FPS: the steps on the forest floor, the
blade cutting the air, the blows, the parries (steel on steel), the
grunts of the orcs and the cry of the demon (a buzzing voice through two
formants), where they happen; the wind and the birds of the forest when
calm, crossfaded to a music when enemies are around.

## Testing it

`tps/tps.spec.ts` tests the parts without a GPU: the heights of the forest
(a flat clearing, a rim), the blows (struck once, the capsule along the
blade), the graph and the clips of the ranger, its lunges (taken out as
root motion under an armature turned and scaled), the bones of the
ragdolls of the monsters, the actions, the graph of the enemies on a fake
game (the roar, the chase, the blow in its window, two striking at once,
the `poise`, the fall), the sounds on a `FakeAudioEngine`, and the
progress shown while loading.

## What we have

- A forest of 2 500 instanced firs and 5 000 clumps of grass, with their
  levels of detail, shadows in cascades and fog, at 60 frames per second
  and more.
- Models prepared from CC0 assets by tools: levels made of cards, clips
  copied by the names of the joints, a sword placed by the pose of a hand.
- A third-person camera that stops at the trunks.
- A ranger whose actions are decided by code and followed by its
  animation graph; blows as volumes, struck once per blow.
- Enemies decided by a behavior graph, compiled to JavaScript:
  surrounding the hero, striking two at a time, walking their paths on a
  navigation mesh around the trees, making way for each other.
- Blows that carry the ranger by root motion, and enemies falling as
  ragdolls where the blow sends them.

Some ideas left for later: getting up from a ragdoll (`Pose.blend` back to
an animation), a ragdoll for the ranger, obstacles placed in the forest
(a [tiled mesh](/fdw-engine/tutorials/crowds/)).
