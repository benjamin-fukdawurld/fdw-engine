Displacement mapping needs vertices: a terrain of a kilometer with a vertex
every 25 cm would have 16 million of them, most of them far away, smaller
than a pixel. **Tessellation** subdivides each part of a surface as much as
its size on the screen asks. Neither WebGPU nor WebGL2 has hardware
tessellation: the engine draws **patches** from templates subdivided 1, 2,
4… 64 times, and generates their vertices in the vertex shader. Below, a
terrain of 32 × 32 patches displaced by a height map; click the canvas to
color the patches by level, from blue (2 triangles) to red (8192).

## A surface of patches

The tessellation is in its own package, `@fdw/engine-tessellation`. A
surface is made of quad **patches**; `patchGrid` makes a flat grid of them,
in the XZ plane, facing +Y, with uvs from 0 to 1:

```ts
import { patchGrid, PatchSurface, patchVertex } from '@fdw/engine-tessellation';

const patches = patchGrid({ width: 1024, depth: 1024, columns: 32, rows: 32 });
```

Its material calls `patchVertex`: in the vertex shader, it finds the patch
and the point of its template drawn, interpolates the corners of the patch,
moves the point along the normal by the height map, and computes the normal
of the displaced surface. It gives the **position**, **normal** and **uv** of
the vertex to the rest of the graph: the surface, the macros and
`g.attribute('uv')` read them as if they came from a mesh.

```ts
const g = new GraphBuilder('terrain');
const vertex = patchVertex(g, {
  displacement: {
    texture: g.texture('heightMap'),
    sampler: g.sampler('heightSampler'),
    scale: 120, // The height of a texel of 1, in meters.
    texel: 1 / 256, // 1 / the size of the height map: for the normal.
  },
});
g.surface({
  shadingModel: 'phong',
  baseColor: colorOf(vertex.height), // The height read, from 0 to 1.
});
const material = new Material({
  graph: g.build(),
  values: { heightMap, heightSampler },
});
```

The surface is a render item. Each frame, `update` chooses the levels for
the camera, then the renderer draws it with the others:

```ts
const terrain = new PatchSurface(backend, {
  patches,
  material, // One material per surface: it holds the patches drawn.
  displacement: 120, // How far the points move: the bounds grow by it.
});

// Each frame:
terrain.update(camera, surface); // The size of the image, in pixels.
renderer.render({ items: [terrain, ...others], camera, surface, lights });
```

## How it works

1. **The templates**: a patch subdivided in 2^level × 2^level quads, from
   level 0 (2 triangles) to 6 (8192). Nothing is stored: a vertex knows its
   place in its template from its index.
2. **The choice of the levels**, on the CPU, each frame: each edge gets the
   level that makes its triangles about `edgePixels` (8) pixels long on the
   screen, seen from its closest point; each patch gets the finest level of
   its four edges. The patches outside the view are left out.
3. **The draw**: the patches are sorted by level and written into a
   texture, one texel each. A single draw, without vertex buffer, generates
   all their vertices: from its index, a vertex finds its level, its patch,
   its quad, its corner.
4. **The evaluation**, in the vertex shader: the corners of the patch are
   read from a texture (`std.textureLoad`), interpolated, displaced by the
   height map (`std.textureSampleLevel`); the normal comes from the slopes
   of the height map.

**No cracks.** Two neighbors at different levels would not have the same
points on their common edge: the fine one would leave gaps along the coarse
one. Each edge has a single level, shared by its two patches; on the finer
patch, the points of the edge move to the grid of the edge's level, and the
two sides have exactly the same points. The patches interpolate their
corners so that a common edge gives the same positions to the bit.

The same path on WebGPU, Vulkan and WebGL2: only vertex shaders and
textures, no compute nor hardware tessellation.

## Computed attributes

What `patchVertex` relies on is a general feature of the material graphs:
`g.vertexAttribute(semantic, value)` computes an attribute instead of
reading it from the mesh. Every read of the attribute, in the graph, in the
vertex stage of the surface and in the macros, gets the value:

```ts
// A vertex placed by its index: the mesh needs no position.
g.vertexAttribute('position', positionFromIndex);
g.vertexAttribute('normal', [0, 1, 0]);
```

The mesh no longer needs these attributes; a mesh without vertex buffers
draws `setVertexCount(count)` vertices, generated in the shader.

## What it costs

The terrain of the demo (`terrain` in the demo app), 32 × 32 patches over a
kilometer, at 1280 × 720 on Vulkan (RTX 2060 SUPER): about 470 patches in
view, **400,000 triangles** adaptive, against 3.8 million with every patch
at the finest level (5 here: the 2^24 vertices of a frame), 1140 frames per
second against 660.

The choice of the levels costs 0.4 ms of CPU for 4096 patches (0.6 ms with
the culling of the patches outside the view), whatever the number of
triangles.

## What to know

- **One update per frame**: the shadow maps and the other views of the
  frame draw the patches chosen for the camera given to `update`.
- **The culled patches cast no shadows**: with a low sun, hills outside the
  view may cast shadows into it; `cull: false` keeps all the patches.
- **The levels**: `edgePixels` (8) sets the density, `minLevel` and
  `maxLevel` (0 and 6) bound it. At most 2^24 vertices a frame: beyond, the
  surface lowers its levels.
- **The normal**: computed from the height map, the one of the vertices
  changes with their level. For a lighting that does not change with the
  level, read the normal per pixel from the height map (the terrain of the
  demo does, see `terrainGraph` in `@fdw/demo-scenes`).
- **Patches of your own**: `new Patches([...])` takes quads by their
  corners (position, normal, uv). Neighbors must share the corners of their
  common edges; in the same direction, the seam is exact.
