A square of ruins, a market and rocks: 1 600 static objects, and no two
alike. Every column, stone, crate, barrel and rock has a mesh of its own.
Instancing draws in one call the objects that share a mesh and a
material (the walls of [the maze](/fdw-engine/game/maze/), the firs of
[the forest](/fdw-engine/tutorials/levels-of-detail/)): here it has
nothing to share, and each object would be a draw call. The **static
batching** merges the static objects of a region and of a material into
one mesh: about 70 calls instead of 1 200.

Click the canvas to switch the batching off and on. Hover an object: its
name comes back from the GPU, batched or not. A fallen drum rolls to and
fro: its batch is not rebuilt, only its vertices are written again.

## Batching a scene

```ts
import { Scene, StaticBatches } from '@fdw/engine-scene';

const scene = new Scene();
// … static nodes: scene.root.add({ static: true }).setMesh(mesh, material)
const batches = new StaticBatches(scene, { cellSize: 40 });
```

That is all: the scene, as the source of the renderer, gives the batches
instead of the objects they hold. `StaticBatches` groups the renderers of
the **static nodes** by the cell of a grid (`cellSize`, in units of the
scene) and by material, and merges each group into one mesh, in world
space. `batches.destroy()` draws them alone again.

What is merged:

- the renderers of static nodes, visible;
- whose mesh has triangles, with its positions, normals and tangents in
  32-bit floats, and no skin (`mergeKey(mesh)` says whether it can be);
- whose material is opaque or masked: the transparent objects are sorted
  one by one, from back to front, and stay alone;
- with the same attributes, layers and shadow (`castShadow`) as the others
  of their group;
- that the `filter` option keeps: leave out the nodes of a
  [level of detail](/fdw-engine/tutorials/levels-of-detail/), which switch
  levels at every frame, and those an editor is about to move.

A group of a single renderer is not merged (`minCount`, 2 by default),
and a group of more than `maxVertices` (262 144) vertices is split.

## When an object changes

An object that **moves in its cell** has its vertices rewritten in
place: the renderer writes only these bytes to the GPU, about 0.03 ms for
an object of the square. A batch is **rebuilt** when one of its objects
leaves its cell, is hidden, removed, given another mesh or material
(`node.setMesh`), other layers, or a mirror more or less (its faces turn
over). The changes are applied at the next flush of the scene, before the
next frame, like the bounds and the spatial index. A system writing the
columns of the renderers directly calls `scene.rendererChanged(entity)`.

Rebuilding a batch is merging its meshes again: about a millisecond for
the hundreds of rocks of a cell, 60 ms for the whole square at the
start. `batches.stats` counts the `rebuilds` and the moves in place
(`updates`). Fine for a door opened from time to time; an object that
moves at every frame is not static, and is better drawn alone.

## Picking in a batch

The renderers of a scene have identifiers: `scene.objectId(entity)`,
the entity plus one (0 is no object). The `'object-id'` outputs of the
scene passes write them for each pixel, as in
[picking](/fdw-engine/tutorials/picking/). A batch is one draw, one row of
the object table: the identifiers of its objects are stored **per
vertex**, in an `objectId` attribute, which the scene passes read instead
of the table.

```ts
const [id] = await pickIds(backend, ids, pixel);
const entity = scene.entityOf(id); // the object, batched or not
const batch = batches.batchOf(entity); // its batch, if any
```

The other queries of the scene (raycasts, volumes) do not see the
batches: they find the objects themselves, which stay in the spatial
index.

## What it costs

- **The geometry, twice on the GPU.** Each batch holds a copy of the
  meshes of its objects, in world space, plus 4 bytes per vertex for the
  identifiers: 5.2 MB for the 4.7 MB of the square. On the CPU, a batch
  gives its copy back once uploaded (`stats.cpuBytes` falls to 0 after the
  first frame); it merges its objects again only if its data is read, by
  another renderer for example. `batches.stats` gives the bytes, the
  batches, the rebuilds and their time.
- **Culling by cell.** A batch is drawn whole as soon as a part of its
  cell is in view, and casts all its shadows. Smaller cells cull finer
  and rebuild less; larger ones draw fewer calls. The occlusion culling
  sees a batch as one object too.
- **World space in the materials.** A batch is placed by the identity:
  its attributes are already in world space. A material computing the
  world position from `object.world` and the position, as most do, sees
  no difference; one reading the position or the normal as those of the
  object does.

## Measures

The square of the example, in the native demo
(`nx run demo-native:start -- decor`, and `decor-no-batching`), without
vsync, on the development machine:

|                             | Alone       | Batched     |
| --------------------------- | ----------- | ----------- |
| Draw calls (the camera)     | about 1 200 | about 70    |
| Vulkan                      | 169 fps     | **833 fps** |
| WebGPU (Dawn)               | 50 fps      | **305 fps** |
| `Renderer.render`, CPU only | 1.57 ms     | **0.14 ms** |

The last line is a benchmark of `engine-scene` (`StaticBatches.bench.ts`):
1 000 objects of their own meshes, in 4 materials, on the fake backend.
The gain comes from the calls saved, not from the GPU: the triangles are
the same.

## Instancing or batching?

- **The same mesh many times** (trees, walls, crowds): instancing,
  automatic, and nothing is copied.
- **Static objects, all different** (a hand-made set, an imported level, a
  CAD assembly): the static batching.
- **Moving objects**: neither merges them; each moving object is a draw,
  or an instance of its mesh.
