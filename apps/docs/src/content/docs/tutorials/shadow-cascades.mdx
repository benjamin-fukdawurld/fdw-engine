An avenue of trees, 300 m long, under a low sun. A single shadow map
covers what the camera sees: from the trees at its feet to the end of the
avenue. Its texels are spread over 300 m, and the shadows near the camera
are blurred. **Cascades** cut the view into slices by depth, each with its
own shadow map: the first covers a few meters, the last the whole
distance. A **fog** hides the end of the avenue.

Click the canvas: **C** switches between one shadow map and four cascades,
**F** turns the fog on and off.

## Cascades on a directional light

```ts
const sun = new DirectionalLight({
  castShadow: true,
  shadow: { cascades: { count: 4, distance: 120 } },
});
```

`shadow.cascades` replaces the single shadow map of a directional light:

- `count`: from 1 to 4 slices (4 by default);
- `distance`: how far from the camera the shadows reach (the far plane of
  the camera by default). They fade out over the last 15 %;
- `lambda`: how the depths of the slices grow, from 0 (even steps) to 1
  (each slice a constant ratio further, as many texels on screen for
  each). 0.75 by default.

With 4 slices up to 120 m, from a camera whose near plane is 0.1 m, the
cascades end at about 8 m, 18 m, 38 m and 120 m: the texels of the first
are about 15 times smaller than those of a map covering the whole
distance.

Each cascade is a tile of the shadow atlas of the renderer: four cascades
in an atlas of 2048 px get 1024 px each.

```ts
const renderer = new Renderer(backend, { shadowAtlasSize: 2048 });
```

One light per frame has cascades: the sun. The other lights casting
shadows keep a tile each. An orthographic camera has no slices: the light
falls back to its single map.

## Stable shadows

A shadow map that follows the camera shimmers: its texels slide over the
ground as the camera moves, and the edges of the shadows crawl. Two
things keep the cascades still:

- the sphere of each slice depends only on the field of view of the
  camera and on the depths of the slice, not on where the camera looks:
  turning the camera does not change the size of the texels;
- the center of each map moves by whole texels in the space of the light.

`cascadeSplits` and `sliceSphere` of `@fdw/engine-core` compute them, for
a tool that wants to show them.

In the shaders, the cascade of a point is chosen by its depth in the view.
Near the end of a cascade, a growing share of the pixels reads the next
one: a dither rather than a seam.

## The fog

```ts
renderer.render({
  source: scene,
  camera,
  surface,
  fog: { color: { r: 0.5, g: 0.57, b: 0.64 }, density: 0.012 },
});
```

The color of each surface moves towards the color of the fog by
1 − e<sup>−density × distance</sup>: at 0.012, 11 % at 10 m, 70 % at
100 m, 95 % at 250 m (about 3 / density). Two options shape it:

- `heightFalloff`: the density falls with the height, by
  e<sup>−falloff × height</sup> above `height`: a fog lying in the valleys,
  thinner over the hills and towards the sky;
- `height`: the base of the fog, where the density is `density` (0 by
  default).

The fog is computed per pixel, before the tone mapping: its color is a
linear color, as the colors of the lights. The materials do not change:
the renderer compiles the fog into the programs of the views that have one.
The background (`clearColor`) is not fogged: give it a color close to that
of the fog, or draw a sky as a mesh, which fades into it.
