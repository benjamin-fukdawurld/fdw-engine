Three characters walk in place until a blow knocks them down. At that
moment, a **ragdoll** takes over from the animation: a body on each bone,
linked by joints, put in the physics world at the pose of the moment, and
moving at the speed of the blow. Each frame, the bodies give a pose back to
the skinning.

Click the canvas: **Space** knocks the next one down, **R** stands them up.

## The bones

```ts
import { Ragdoll, type RagdollBone } from '@fdw/engine-ragdoll';

const bones: RagdollBone[] = [
  { joint: 'hips', to: 'spine', radius: 0.13 },
  { joint: 'spine', to: 'neck', radius: 0.13, cone: 0.6, twist: [-0.4, 0.4] },
  { joint: 'head', radius: 0.12, cone: 0.7, twist: [-0.6, 0.6] },
  {
    joint: 'upperArm.L',
    to: 'lowerArm.L',
    radius: 0.05,
    cone: 1.4,
    twist: [-0.8, 0.8],
  },
  {
    joint: 'lowerArm.L',
    to: 'hand.L',
    radius: 0.045,
    link: 'hinge',
    limits: [-2.4, 0.2],
  },
  {
    joint: 'upperLeg.L',
    to: 'lowerLeg.L',
    radius: 0.075,
    cone: 1,
    twist: [-0.4, 0.4],
  },
  {
    joint: 'lowerLeg.L',
    to: 'foot.L',
    radius: 0.06,
    link: 'hinge',
    limits: [-0.1, 2.4],
  },
  // … and the right side.
];

const ragdoll = new Ragdoll(world, skeleton, { bones, groups: 2, mask: 1 });
```

A bone is a body on a joint: a **capsule** from the joint to the joint
given by `to`, or a **sphere** on it (a head, a hand). Not every joint
needs one: the chest and the neck here follow the spine, the hands their
forearms, as they were when the ragdoll took over.

Each body hangs on the body of its nearest ancestor: by a **ball** joint (a
shoulder, a hip) or a **hinge** (an elbow, a knee). The collision groups
keep the bodies of the ragdoll from colliding with each other: group 2,
colliding with group 1 only (the ground).

## Limits

A joint without limits lets a limb turn all the way round: a leg folded
along the back, a head facing behind. Each link has its own:

- A **hinge** turns around its `axis`, in the space of the joint (x by
  default), within `limits` in radians, around its angle at the
  activation.
- A **ball** stays in a **cone**: `cone` is its half-angle in radians,
  around the bone as it pointed at the activation (from its joint to `to`,
  or from the joint of the ancestor; `axis` to give another); `twist`
  bounds how far it turns around it. A shoulder leans 80° whichever way,
  and twists 45°.

The cones are an addition of this page to the [physics
API](/fdw-engine/guides/physics/#joints): `cone` on a joint of type
`ball`, `setCone` to change it. Rapier limits each axis of a joint on its
own, a square rather than a circle; before each step, the binding fits the
square to the circle where the joint is.

A rig whose feet are not under its legs — moved by inverse kinematics,
under the root — hangs them on the lower legs with `attach: 'lowerLeg.L'`:
the body of a bone then hangs on the body of that joint rather than on its
ancestor's.

The ragdoll is written against the [abstract physics
API](/fdw-engine/tutorials/physics/): any engine behind it, Rapier here.

## Taking over

```ts
// The node of the skeleton: the parent of its root joint, the root of
// the instance of the model.
const frame = character.root.worldMatrix;
ragdoll.activate(animator.pose, frame, { x: 0.5, y: 1.5, z: -4 });
```

`activate` puts the bodies in the world where the pose puts the joints —
the character falls as it stood, mid-step — moving at the velocity given:
the blow. `deactivate` takes them out.

## The pose of the bodies

```ts
const pose = skeleton.rest.clone();

// Each frame, after the step of the world:
if (ragdoll.active) {
  skinning.applyPose(ragdoll.readPose(pose));
} else {
  skinning.applyPose(animator.update(dt));
}
```

`readPose` turns the poses of the bodies into the local transforms of the
joints, relative to the node of the character at the activation: the node
stays where it was, the body falls away from it. The pose is an ordinary
`Pose`: `Pose.blend(ragdollPose, animator.pose, w, out)` fades from the
ragdoll back to an animation, for a character getting up.
