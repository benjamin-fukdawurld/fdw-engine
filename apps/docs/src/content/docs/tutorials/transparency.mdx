A transparent surface lets what is behind it show through. The engine has
two ways to draw one, chosen by the `alphaMode` of the render state of the
material:

| `alphaMode` | For                             | How                                                  |
| ----------- | ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| `'opaque'`  | everything else (the default)   | the alpha is ignored                                 |
| `'mask'`    | foliage, fences, cut-out shapes | each pixel is either drawn or not, by its alpha      |
| `'blend'`   | glass, water, smoke             | the color is mixed with what is behind, by its alpha |

The example shows both: three panes of colored glass, a glass ball, a
fence with holes, and two crossing panes. The camera turns around them.

## The alpha of the surface

The surface of a material has an `alpha` input, from 0 (invisible) to 1
(opaque), 1 by default. It comes from a uniform, a texture, any value of
the graph:

```ts
const g = new GraphBuilder('glass');
const color = g.uniform('color', BuiltinTypes.vec4f);
g.surface({
  shadingModel: 'phong',
  baseColor: color.swizzle('rgb'),
  alpha: color.swizzle('a'),
});
```

The alpha only matters with a render state that uses it.

## Blended materials

With `alphaMode: 'blend'`, the color of the surface is mixed with the color
already drawn behind it: `color × alpha + behind × (1 − alpha)`.

```ts
const red = new Material({
  graph: g.build(),
  values: { color: [1, 0.2, 0.2, 0.4] },
  renderState: { alphaMode: 'blend' },
});
```

The result depends on **the order** of the draws: what is behind must be
drawn first. The renderer does it:

1. the opaque and masked objects first, in any order (the depth buffer
   sorts them);
2. then the blended objects, **from the farthest to the nearest**, by the
   distance from the camera to their origin, at every frame.

The blended objects **do not write the depth** (`depthWrite: false` by
default for them): a pane must not hide the pane behind it, drawn after it
when the camera turns. They are still tested against the depth of the
opaque objects: a wall in front of a glass hides it.

In the example, the three panes on the left are drawn in a new order as
the camera turns around them: the colors always mix right.

### Double-sided objects

A closed transparent object, a glass ball, shows its back faces through
its front faces. With `doubleSided: true`, the renderer draws it **twice**:
its back faces first, then its front faces over them. For a convex shape
(a ball, a box), the order is then right.

```ts
const ball = new Material({
  graph: g.build(),
  values: { color: [1, 0.85, 0.3, 0.35] },
  renderState: { alphaMode: 'blend', doubleSided: true },
});
```

A single-sided transparent object shows only the faces turned towards the
camera: right for a pane seen from one side, wrong for a ball.

## Masked materials

A fence, the leaves of a tree, a grid: the holes are fully transparent, the
rest fully opaque. With `alphaMode: 'mask'`, the pixels whose alpha is
below `alphaCutoff` (0.5 by default, a value of the material) are
**discarded**; the others are drawn as opaque.

```ts
const texel = g.call(std.textureSample, {
  texture: g.texture('map'),
  sampler: g.sampler('mapSampler'),
  uv,
});
g.surface({
  shadingModel: 'phong',
  baseColor: texel.swizzle('rgb'),
  // 0 in the holes of the texture.
  alpha: texel.swizzle('a'),
});
const fence = new Material({
  graph: g.build(),
  values: { map, mapSampler, alphaCutoff: 0.5 },
  renderState: { alphaMode: 'mask', doubleSided: true },
});
```

Masked materials need no sorting: they are drawn with the opaque ones, and
write the depth. They **cast shadows with their holes**: the shadow maps
discard the same pixels. They also write the outputs of the scene passes
(normals, identifiers: see [Picking](/fdw-engine/tutorials/picking/)),
which the blended ones do not.

Their edges are sharp, and shimmer when the texture is far: the alpha of
the mipmaps averages the holes and the bars. Multisampling smooths the edges
of the triangles, not those of the mask.

## What sorting cannot do

The renderer sorts **objects**, by their origin, not pixels. Some cases have
no right order:

- **Crossing objects**: in the example, the two crossing panes on the
  right. On one side of the crossing the orange pane is in front, on the
  other side the purple one: whichever is drawn last covers the other
  everywhere. Split them in pieces that do not cross.
- **Large objects near small ones**: a large water surface and a fish in
  it; the origin of the water may be closer to the camera than the fish
  while most of the water is behind it. Split the large object, or move its
  origin.
- **Concave objects** (a glass cup): their faces cover each other in an
  order that depends on the view. Split them in convex parts.

When the colors only add up (light, fire, glows), the order does not
matter: such materials need an additive blend, which the render state does
not offer yet.

The exact solutions, order-independent transparency (weighted blended,
per-pixel lists), are not in the engine.

## Transparency and the other passes

- **HDR**: by default, the renderer draws in linear colors in an
  `rgba16float` target, where the colors blend right, then encodes them
  (see [Colors and HDR](/fdw-engine/guides/colors-and-hdr/)).
- **Scene outputs**: the blended objects only write the color, not the
  other outputs; a glass is not seen by picking, nor by the ambient
  occlusion.
- **Deferred rendering**: the blended objects are drawn after the lighting
  pass, in forward (see
  [Deferred rendering](/fdw-engine/tutorials/deferred-rendering/)).
- **Shadows**: the blended objects cast none.
