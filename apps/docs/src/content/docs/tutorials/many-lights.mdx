A square at night, lit by 400 colored lamps drifting above the ground.
There is no limit to the number of lights of a frame: every light whose
range reaches the view is used. But a pixel does not sum them all: the
renderer cuts the view into **clusters**, and each pixel only sums the
lights of its own.

Click the canvas: **C** switches between the clusters and every lamp
for every pixel, to compare their cost.

## Many lights, as usual

Nothing changes in the code: the lights are given to the frame, or come
from the nodes of a scene.

```ts
const lamps = Array.from(
  { length: 400 },
  () =>
    new PointLight({
      diffuse: { x: 1, y: 0.4, z: 0.1 },
      quadraticAttenuation: 28,
    }),
);
renderer.render({ items, camera, surface, lights: [moon, ...lamps] });
```

`renderer.stats.get('scene').lights` tells how many were used: those
whose range reaches the frustum of the camera.

## The range of a light

A point or spot light is attenuated by `1 / (constant + linear × d +
quadratic × d²)`. Its **range** is where its light falls below 1/256 of
its strongest color (`lightRange(light)`): beyond it, the light is not
summed. Give the lamps of a dense scene a strong quadratic attenuation:
the shorter their range, the fewer per pixel.

- A light without attenuation (`constant` 1, the others 0) reaches
  everything: it is summed by every pixel, like a directional light.
- The tails of the attenuation beyond the range are dropped: less than a
  step of an 8-bit color per light, but hundreds of lamps around a point
  can add up to a few percent of light.

## The clusters

The view is cut into 16 × 9 tiles of the screen and 24 slices of depth,
thinner near the camera: 3 456 clusters. Each frame, the renderer lists
in each cluster the point and spot lights that can reach it (a sphere
around each light, around the cone of a spot), on the CPU: about 0.8 ms
for 1 000 lights. The lights are in a storage buffer (the light pool),
the lists in another, on every backend (WebGL2 reads them from data
textures).

A pixel finds its cluster from the position of its point, then sums:

1. the **global** lights: the directional ones, and those without range;
2. the lights of the list of its cluster.

The cost of a pixel depends on the lights around it, not on the number
of lights of the frame. `new Renderer(backend, { lightClusters: false })`
turns them off: each pixel sums every light, as the example does with
**C**.

## In a custom shading model

`light.sum` uses the clusters when it is given the **position** of the
point lit (in the world). The `phong` and `pbr` models give it; a custom
model should too:

```ts
const received = f.call(stdLights.sum, {
  value: f.call(std.mul, { a: light.diffuse, b: nDotL }),
  position,
});
```

Without a position, the sum loops over every light of the frame.

In the loop of a clustered sum, each pixel goes through its own number
of lights: the derivatives between neighboring pixels are undefined
there. The compiler refuses `std.textureSample` (and `dpdx`, `dpdy`,
`fwidth`) in it: sample at an explicit level with
`std.textureSampleLevel`, or compute the value outside the loop.

## Shadows

Each light casting shadows still draws its shadow map into the atlas:
give shadows to a few lights (the sun, a few spots), not to hundreds.
