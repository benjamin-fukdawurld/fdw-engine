The waves of [the sea](/fdw-engine/tutorials/ocean/) are drawn on the GPU;
the physics runs on the CPU. To float, a body needs **the height of the
water** under each of its points. The `Ocean` gives it, and `Buoyancy`
turns it into forces.

<Example name="coast" />

Drag to turn around, the wheel to zoom, WASD to move; **G** switches
between the ocean spectrum and Gerstner waves, **Space** drops a crate,
**R** starts again.

## The height of the water

```ts
const height = ocean.heightAt(x, z);
```

The waves move the water sideways as well as up: the water above (x, z)
comes from another point of the still surface, x0, moved by dx(x0) to x.
`heightAt` finds it by a few fixed point iterations (x0 = x − dx(x0)),
then reads its height. `sampleAt(x, z)` also gives the normal and the
jacobian there.

Where the heights come from depends on the waves:

- **Gerstner waves** are evaluated exactly on the CPU, at the time of the
  frame: the same formula as the vertex shader.
- **The ocean spectrum** lives on the GPU. After each `update`, its
  displacement buffer is **read back** (`buffer.read()`), without waiting:
  the physics uses the heights of the frame before, one frame late, a few
  millimeters off. Until the first read arrives, the CPU computes the same
  FFTs itself (`evaluateSpectrum`, also the path of a server without GPU:
  `readback: false`).

## Archimedes, point by point

```ts
import { boxPoints, Buoyancy } from '@fdw/engine-ocean';

const buoyancy = new Buoyancy(ocean); // anything with heightAt(x, z)

const body = physics.add(node, {
  type: 'dynamic',
  colliders: [{ shape: Shapes.box(0.5), density: 450 }],
});
buoyancy.add(body, {
  points: boxPoints({ x: 0.5, y: 0.5, z: 0.5 }), // 8 points
  volume: 1, // m³
});

buoyancy.install(scheduler); // the fixed step, before 'physics'
physics.install(scheduler);
```

At each fixed step, each point of a body stands for an equal share of its
volume. Below the surface, it pushes the body up by the weight of the
water it displaces (ρ g V, sea water: 1025 kg/m³), at the point: a crate
half under water gets half of its push, and a body tilted by a wave gets
more push on its low side, which rights it. The water also **drags** each
wet point against its velocity (`drag`, per second), which damps the
bobbing and the spinning.

A crate of wood (450 kg/m³) floats with a little less than half of it
under water. The boat is a convex hull (its collider, from the points of
its shell), its buoyancy points a lattice filling the hull up to the
gunwale:

```ts
const boat = createBoat(); // @fdw/demo-scenes
const body = physics.add(node, {
  type: 'dynamic',
  colliders: [
    // A light hull: 150 kg.
    { shape: Shapes.convex(boat.hull), density: 50 },
    // The rowers and their gear, low in the boat: 300 kg.
    { shape: Shapes.box(0.3, 0.05, 0.9), position: { y: -0.3 }, density: 2800 },
  ],
  angularDamping: 1.5,
});
buoyancy.add(body, {
  points: boat.floats, // a lattice inside the hull
  volume: boat.volume,
  depth: boat.depth, // the height of a cell of the lattice
  drag: 3,
});
```

Three things keep a boat upright and calm:

- **Points through the whole hull**, not only along its bottom: heeled,
  the boat dips more of its volume on its low side, and the water rights
  it. Points along the bottom alone push the same way whatever the
  heel: the boat capsizes as soon as a wave rolls it over.
- **A low center of mass**: a hull alone is light and its center high (its
  sides, its gunwale). The weight of the crew, low in the boat (an extra
  collider, heavy and small), brings it under the water.
- **Damping**: a real hull loses its energy in the waves it makes. `drag`
  (per second, at each wet point) damps the bobbing, `angularDamping` the
  rolling; without them, the boat keeps swinging and jumps on the short
  waves.

Sleeping bodies on the water are woken up: the waves move under them.

## The coast

The terrain is that of the [tessellation](/fdw-engine/tutorials/tessellation/):
heights from 0 to 1 (a beach, hills, a headland, the sea floor going down),
a `PatchSurface` for the GPU, a heightfield for the physics. The sea is at
12 m (`level`). The `OceanRenderer` draws the coast first, with its
depth: through the water, the sand shows in the shallows and fades into
the color of the sea as it gets deeper, and foam runs along the shore,
where the water is thin.

```ts
ocean.update(camera.position, time);
terrain.surface.update(camera, surface);
oceanRenderer.render({
  ocean,
  source: scene, // the crates, the boat, the light
  items: [terrain.surface],
  camera,
  surface,
  time,
});
```
