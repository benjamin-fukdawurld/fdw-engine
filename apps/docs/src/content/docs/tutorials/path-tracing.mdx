The rasterization lights each point from the lights alone, plus a flat
ambient light. Light bounces, though: the red wall tints the floor, the
ceiling lit by the floor lights the room. A **path tracer** follows the
light backwards: from the camera, through each pixel, bouncing off the
surfaces until it finds a light.

<Example name="path-tracing" />

The image starts grainy and clears up as the frames add up. Click the
canvas to compare with the rasterization of the same scene.

## The path tracer

```ts
import { PathTracer, RayScene } from '@fdw/engine-raytracing';

const tracer = new PathTracer(renderer, new RayScene(backend), {
  bounces: 4,
  sky: { zenith, horizon, ground }, // what the rays lost see
});

onFrame((time) => {
  tracer.scene.update({ items });
  tracer.render({ camera, surface, lights: [light], time });
});
```

Same scene, same materials as the rasterization: a material compiled for
the points hit by rays gives the parameters of the BSDF of the `pbr`
model (base color, metalness, roughness, normal, emission); `unlit`
materials are light sources (their color is their emission).

## A frame

All the pixels at once, in waves of compute passes:

1. **The rays of the camera**, through a random point of each pixel (the
   antialiasing comes with the frames).
2. For each bounce: **trace** the rays (the closest hit); **the surfaces**
   hit, one dispatch per material of the scene; then **the bounce**: the
   emission of the surface, the light of one light chosen at random (a
   shadow ray towards it), and the next direction, sampled from the BSDF
   (a diffuse or a specular lobe, the weight of the path adjusted by the
   density). After two bounces, the paths that carry little light end at
   random (the Russian roulette). The masked and blended pixels let the
   ray go through.
3. **The accumulation**: the frame added to the sum of those before, while
   the camera, the scene, the materials and the lights stay; the average
   is the image.
4. **The filter**: while the frames are few, an à-trous filter smooths the
   noise across the pixels that share a surface (close normals and
   depths), dividing by the albedo so that the textures stay sharp.

## The noise

A path finds the light by chance: where the light is hard to reach, the
first frames are grainy.

- **Lights** (`lights`) are reached on purpose, by a shadow ray: they make
  little noise. An emissive surface is only found by bouncing into it: a
  small and bright one makes fireflies. Light a lamp with a light where it
  is (the example puts a point light under its panel).
- `maxRadiance` (8) clamps the brightest samples: less noise, a little
  less light.
- More frames: the average converges, about the square root of their
  number.

When the camera moves, the gathering starts again; the path tracer suits a
still view, a preview, a picture, more than a game.
