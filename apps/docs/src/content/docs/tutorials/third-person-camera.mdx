A ranger among pillars, seen from behind, over its right shoulder. The
mouse turns the camera around it; walking, the ranger goes where the
camera looks. When a pillar comes between them, the camera moves in at
once, then back out smoothly once the way is clear.

Click the canvas: the **mouse** turns the camera, **W A S D** (Z Q S D
on AZERTY) walks, the **wheel** brings the camera closer. With a gamepad:
the left stick walks, the right stick turns. The line under the canvas
shows the length of the arm.

## The operator

```ts
import { ThirdPersonActions, ThirdPersonOperator } from '@fdw/engine-camera';

const actions = new ActionMap(input, ThirdPersonActions);
const operator = new ThirdPersonOperator(camera, actions, {
  distance: 4,
  height: 1.6,
  shoulder: 0.5,
});

// Each frame, once the character has moved:
operator.focus = characterFeet;
operator.update(dt);
```

`ThirdPersonOperator` is an operator of `@fdw/engine-camera`, like the
orbit and the flight of [Camera operators](/fdw-engine/tutorials/camera-operators/):
it reads its actions, and moves a camera (or anything with a position and
a rotation). Its actions:

- `look`: the arrows and the right stick turn the camera;
- `drag` and `pointer`: the pointer turns it while locked, or while the
  secondary button is down;
- `zoomStep`: the wheel brings it closer, between `minDistance` and
  `maxDistance`.

What it places:

- the **pivot**, `height` above the `focus` (the head above the feet). The
  pivot **follows** the focus with a damped spring: it covers half of its
  way in `followHalfLife` seconds (0.06 by default). The camera does not
  jolt at each step of the character;
- the **arm**, from the pivot backwards along the view, `distance` long;
- the **shoulder**: the camera `shoulder` meters to the right of the arm
  (negative: the left). The character stands to the left of the center of
  the view, out of the way of what it looks at.

The pitch is bounded (`minPitch`, `maxPitch`: from −70° to 45° by
default): the camera does not go under the ground, nor over the head.

## The walls: a probe

```ts
const operator = new ThirdPersonOperator(camera, actions, {
  probe: (from, to, radius) =>
    world.shapeCast(
      Shapes.sphere(radius),
      from,
      { x: 0, y: 0, z: 0, w: 1 },
      directionFrom(from, to),
      distanceBetween(from, to),
      { exclude: character.body },
    )?.distance,
});
```

The camera must not go through a pillar: the operator asks a **probe**
how far a sphere of `radius` (0.25 m) moves from the pivot towards the
camera before it hits something. The probe is a function of the
application: `engine-camera` knows nothing of the physics. Here, it casts a
sphere in the world of [the physics](/fdw-engine/tutorials/physics/), the
body of the character excluded; a game without physics would cast a ray
in its scene.

- **Shorter at once**: when the probe hits closer than the arm, the arm
  shortens at once — never a frame with a pillar between the camera and
  the character.
- **Longer smoothly**: when the way clears, the arm grows back by half of
  what is left in `returnHalfLife` seconds (0.25).
- The shoulder shortens with the arm: close to a wall, the camera stays
  behind the head.

`operator.arm` gives the length of the arm now; `operator.snap()` puts the
pivot on the focus at once, after a teleport.

## Walking where the camera looks

```ts
const move = actions.vector('move', { x: 0, y: 0 });
const { forward, right } = operator; // on the ground
const direction = {
  x: forward.x * move.y + right.x * move.x,
  z: forward.z * move.y + right.z * move.x,
};
character.move({
  x: direction.x * speed * dt,
  y: fall * dt,
  z: direction.z * speed * dt,
});
```

`forward` and `right` are the directions of the view on the ground: up on
the stick goes away from the camera, whatever its pitch. The character is
a `PhysicsCharacter` (see [the physics](/fdw-engine/tutorials/physics/)): it
slides along the pillars and climbs small steps. Its model is a child of
its node, turned towards where it walks; its animation blends the idle,
the walk and the jog by its speed (see
[An animated character](/fdw-engine/tutorials/character-animation/)).

The order in a frame matters: the character moves, then the camera follows
it **as drawn** — after the bodies are placed — so that the character
never trembles in the view.
