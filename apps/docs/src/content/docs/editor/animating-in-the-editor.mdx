A character is animated by an [animation graph](/fdw-engine/guides/animation/):
clips, blends, layers, and state machines driven by parameters (the speed,
on the ground, a jump). The **Animation graph** panel edits the graphs of
the scene while they play: that of the hero of the
[third-person game](/fdw-engine/game/tps/), and those of the orcs and of the
demon.

The editor below runs the game, the panel beside it (**View → Animation
graph**). Click the viewport to play, move with **WASD**, run with
**Shift**, jump with **Space**.

## 1. What plays

The list of the bar has the animation graphs of the scene: **hero
animation**, then those of the kinds of enemies (each enemy spawned plays
the graph of its kind, those spawned later the graph edited). While the
game runs, the canvas shows, for the animator followed:

- on each node its **weight** in the pose, a bar under its title and a
  badge (`62%`); a node that does not play is dimmed;
- on a state machine its **state**, and the state it fades to (`idle →
run`).

The **Play** tab chooses the animator followed (an orc among the others),
lists the **parameters** with their values, and forces them: a number, a
boolean, a trigger fired. The game sets its parameters at each frame: a
value forced holds until it does (stop moving the hero to see a speed
forced take effect).

## 2. The state machine

A double click on a **state machine** node opens it in a view of its own:

- the **states** are boxes, the initial one marked, the state playing in
  green, the one it fades to in amber with the progress of the fade;
- the **transitions** are arrows: the one in progress in amber; a click
  selects one, **Delete** removes it;
- a drag from the blue dot of a state to another makes a transition;
  **Any state** is the source of the transitions from any state (`*`).

The **State machine** tab edits it: the states (renamed with their
transitions and their input, added, removed, the initial one) and each
transition: its states, its fade in seconds, its exit time (in cycles of
the state), its trigger, its conditions on the parameters (`speed > 1.2`).
Each change is a command of the history (**Ctrl+Z**), and runs at once in
the animators: their parameters keep their values, their machines start
again in their initial states.

The states are inputs of the node: a state added needs a pose, connected
in the graph (a clip, a blend), else the graph has an error and the
animators keep the last graph without one. A graph that names a clip the
character does not have is not played either; the panel says why
(**Not running: …**).

## 3. The graph

Outside of the machine, the graph is edited as the
[materials](/fdw-engine/editor/editing-a-material/): **Space** adds a clip,
a blend, a blend space, a layer, a parameter; the wires carry poses,
numbers and booleans; the **Node** tab sets the clip of a node, its speed,
its loop, the points of a blend space. **Save** writes a
`.animation.json`, a resource of the engine (`AnimationGraphType`).
