The materials of the engine are graphs ([Material graphs](/fdw-engine/guides/material-graph/)).
The editor edits them with the mouse, in the **Material graph** panel: the
same editor of graphs as the animations and the behaviors, each kind of
graph a plugin of the editor. This tutorial changes the wood of the
gallery of [procedural materials](/fdw-engine/tutorials/procedural-materials/),
while it is drawn.

The editor below shows the gallery, the panel opened beside the viewport
(**View → Material graph**, or **Graphs: Edit the materials** in the
palette). Nothing is kept when the page is left.

## 1. The graph of the scene

The list at the left of the bar has the materials of the scene shown: the
gallery gives each object's (wood, marble, rock, lava, bricks, tiles,
baked rock), the level its own. The first one opens by itself; **live**
says that each change that compiles is drawn in the scene at once, the
material made again with the values of the old one.

The canvas moves like the viewport:

| Input                           | What it does                                    |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Middle or right button, dragged | Moves the view                                  |
| Wheel                           | Zooms around the pointer                        |
| **F**, the frame button         | Frames the selection, or the whole graph        |
| Click, Shift+click, Ctrl+click  | Selects, adds, toggles                          |
| Drag on the background          | Selects in a rectangle                          |
| Drag on a node                  | Moves the selection                             |
| **Space** or **Tab**            | Searches a node to add, at the pointer          |
| **Ctrl+F**                      | Finds a node of the graph                       |
| **Delete**                      | Removes the selection (nodes, wires, notes)     |
| **Ctrl+C**, **X**, **V**, **D** | Copy, cut, paste, duplicate                     |
| **Ctrl+Z**, **Ctrl+Y**          | Undo, redo (also in the bar)                    |
| **N**, **Ctrl+G**               | A note at the pointer, a group of the selection |

The keys go to the canvas once it is clicked. The arrangement button
places the nodes again in columns, from the inputs to the output: the
materials made in code have no positions, and are arranged so when opened.

## 2. Nodes and wires

A node has its inputs on the left, its outputs on the right, each with its
type: `f32`, `vec3<f32>`… An input not connected shows its value, in gray.
Drag from a port to another to connect them: while dragging, the ports
that take the wire grow. The engine checks the connection against the
graph: a `vec4<f32>` into a `vec3<f32>` is **refused**, and the message of
the validation shows at the bottom of the canvas. The only conversion is a
scalar into a vector.

**Alt+click** on a port removes its wires. A wire dropped in the void
opens the search, filtered on the nodes that take it: the node chosen is
added there, connected.

Try it on the wood:

1. Press **Space** over the canvas, type `smoothstep`, Enter.
2. Drag the output of a `mul` node into its `x` input, and its output
   into a `mix` of the color. The sphere of the preview and the capsule of
   the gallery change.
3. **Ctrl+Z** undoes the change; each one is a command of the history of
   the graph (P15), the labels in the tooltips of the bar.

## 3. The inspector and the problems

The column at the right of the panel has tabs:

- **Node**: the node selected, its label, its parameters (the `type` of a
  uniform among the types of the shaders) and the values of its inputs not
  connected, a field per component for the vectors. A value is applied by
  Enter, or by leaving the field.
- **Problems**: the errors and warnings of the graph; a click frames the
  node. A node in error has a red border, its message in its tooltip. The
  bar counts them.

A graph with an error is not drawn: the scene keeps the last material that
compiled, until the graph compiles again.

## 4. The preview and the code

- **Preview**: the material alone, on a sphere, a cube or a plane, drawn by
  the engine into a texture and read back, recompiled at each change. With
  **Animate**, it plays the time of the engine (the lava flows). A material
  of a file or a new one, without a scene, is seen there.
- **Code**: what the engine draws, generated for its backend (WGSL on
  WebGPU, GLSL ES on WebGL2, HLSL on Vulkan), with the time of the
  compilation. Its **source map** links the nodes to the lines: the lines of
  the node selected stand out, a click on a line selects its node. The
  nodes laid by a macro (the octaves of a `fbm`) are those of the macro.

## 5. Files, notes and groups

- **New**, **Open**, **Save** (the bar): a new material, a
  `.material.json` of the user, the graph saved as JSON. A graph saved is a
  resource of the engine (`MaterialGraphType`), loaded by a store.
- **N** lays a note at the pointer: double-click it to write in it. **Ctrl+G**
  groups the nodes selected in a frame, moved with them; double-click its
  title to rename it, **Ctrl+Shift+G** ungroups.
- Notes, groups and positions are saved in the document (`layout`),
  ignored by the compiler.

The graph is edited in the engine, which may run in another process: the
panel shows its projection and sends commands, as the outliner does for
the level. [Debugging a behavior](/fdw-engine/editor/debugging-a-behavior/)
uses the same editor on the mind of the enemies of the game.
