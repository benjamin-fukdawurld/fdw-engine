This tutorial goes through the editor with the level it opens first: a
ground, cubes, spheres, a table and the sun. Everything happens in the
editor below; nothing is kept when the page is left, so try freely.

## 1. The layout

The editor is laid out like the main window of a desktop application:

```
┌─────────────────────────── menu bar ───────────────────────────┐
├────────────────────────── toolbars, top ───────────────────────┤
│ toolbars │ left dock │   central area   │ right dock │ toolbars │
│          │           ├──────────────────┤            │          │
│          │           │   bottom dock    │            │          │
├───────────────────────── toolbars, bottom ─────────────────────┤
└────────────────────────── status bar ──────────────────────────┘
```

- **The central area** holds the viewport. Its tab can be split or moved
  in the area, and a group can float above it.
- **The docks** hold the panels: the **Outliner** (the tree of the level)
  on the left, the **Inspector** on the right, the **Console** (the
  messages of the engine) at the bottom; next to the Inspector, the
  Statistics of the plugin of [Writing a plugin](/fdw-engine/editor/writing-a-plugin/)
  (the Marks menu is that of the sample plugin). Drag a tab to reorder it, or to
  split its dock. The buttons at the left of the top toolbar show and hide
  the docks; the **View** menu shows the panels, the toolbars, and resets
  the layout.
- **The status bar** tells the backend and the device, the frames per
  second with the CPU and GPU time of a frame, the size of the viewport,
  the name of the level (`•` once changed), the mode and the tools active.
- **The command palette** (**Ctrl+Shift+P** or **F1**) lists every
  command enabled, with its key: type a few letters of its name.

The editor keeps its layout between sessions (the desktop one in its user
data, the web one in IndexedDB); not in the pages of this site, which start
from the default one.

## 2. Moving around

The camera turns around a point of the level:

| Input                  | What it does                            |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Right button, dragged  | Turns around the point                  |
| Middle button, dragged | Pans                                    |
| Wheel                  | Zooms                                   |
| **F**                  | Frames the selection (**Edit → Frame**) |

A drag goes on outside of the viewport: the pointer stays captured until
the button is released.

## 3. Selecting

The **main button** belongs to the tool of the editor (below). With any of
them:

- a **click** selects the object under the pointer, a click in the void
  clears the selection; **Shift** adds to it, **Ctrl** toggles, **Alt**
  removes;
- a **drag** from the void draws a rectangle: the objects inside are
  selected;
- in the **Outliner**, a click selects too (Shift, Ctrl as well), a double
  click frames, the eye hides and shows.

A click in the viewport selects a whole object under the root: the table,
not one of its legs. To reach a leg, open the table in the Outliner and
click the leg there.

The selected objects are outlined, the one under the pointer too, more
faintly. The **active** object is the last selected: the Inspector shows
it.

## 4. Moving, turning, scaling

The tools are in the top toolbar and in the **Edit** menu:

| Key   | Tool   | The gizmo                                        |
| ----- | ------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Q** | Select | None: the main button selects only               |
| **W** | Move   | Arrows along the axes, squares for the planes    |
| **E** | Rotate | Rings around the axes                            |
| **R** | Scale  | Handles along the axes, the center for all three |

Select the blue cube, press **W**, and drag its red arrow: the cube moves
along X. Each drag is one step of the history. Three toggles change the
gizmo:

- **X**: the axes of the world, or those of the object;
- **C**: the pivot, at the center of the selection, or at the origin of
  each object;
- the magnet: snapping, by half a meter, 15 degrees, a quarter of scale.

## 5. The Inspector

The Inspector edits the active object: its name, its visibility, its
position, rotation (in degrees) and scale, and the numbers of its
components. A field is applied by **Enter**, or by leaving it; the
consecutive changes of one field make one step of the history.

## 6. Adding, removing, reorganizing

- **Edit → Add** adds an empty node, a cube, a sphere or a point light at
  the center of the level, selected: move it with **W**.
- **Delete** removes the selection.
- In the Outliner, drag an object onto another to put it under it: it
  moves with its new parent.

## 7. Undoing

Every change is a command of the history: **Ctrl+Z** undoes, **Ctrl+Y**
(or **Ctrl+Shift+Z**) redoes. The **Edit** menu tells the next step of
each. Selecting is not in the history, as in most editors.

## 8. Saving and opening

The **File** menu:

- **Save** (**Ctrl+S**) writes the level as a scene of the engine
  (`.scene.json`, its buffers in it), the format of
  [saving scenes](/fdw-engine/tutorials/saving-scenes/);
- **Export as glTF** writes a `.glb`, for the other tools;
- **Open** (**Ctrl+O**) reads a scene of the engine or a glTF file
  (`.glb`, or `.gltf` with its buffers in it);
- **New** goes back to the sample level.

In the browser, Chromium opens a dialog to choose where to write; the other
browsers download the file. On the desktop, the dialogs are those of the
system.

## 9. Rendering with rays

**Edit → Rendering** chooses how the viewport draws the level:
**Rasterized** (the default), **Ray traced shadows**, **Ray traced
reflections** (the shadows too), or **Path traced**: the whole image by
rays, clearing up while the camera stays still (see
[ray tracing](/fdw-engine/guides/ray-tracing/)). The command palette has
them too. The renderings by rays need a backend that traces them: Vulkan
on the desktop, WebGPU in the browser; on WebGL2 they are disabled. The
choice is kept with the layout. Selecting, the outlines and the gizmos
work in every rendering.

## 10. The other scenes

The **Scene** menu shows the demo scenes in the viewport instead of the
level: physics, the ocean, the game in the forest… They run with the
inputs of the game: click the viewport, then play. **Scene → level** comes
back to the level, as it was left.
